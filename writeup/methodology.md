## 1. The question

- "Who will hit better over the rest of the season?" is a question every
  front office answers daily; this is a public, reproducible answer.
- One-sentence version of the method: start from what a player did over the
  last three years, then update it with what he's done this season —
  trusting the new data exactly as much as its sample size earns, per skill.

## 2. Why shrinkage (the intuition section)

- The 50-PA hot streak problem. Two players, same .400 wOBA in 60 PA;
  one is a career .380, one a career .290. They are not the same bet.
- Different skills stabilize at different speeds: strikeouts speak in ~60
  PA, walks ~120, homers ~170, doubles/triples never finish speaking within
  one season. [Chart: shrinkage weights by event over PA.]

## 3. The system

- Prior: Marcel — 5/4/3 weighted last three seasons, regressed to the
  league with 1,200 PA, age adjustment. Computable in March.
- Update: per-component empirical-Bayes blend; recombine with the season's
  wOBA weights.
- Why event rates instead of the usual BB%/K%/ISO/BABIP: those four don't
  add up to wOBA; the seven PA outcomes do. (One sentence — the audit has
  the details.)
- Uncertainty: every projection ships with a predictive interval; the
  interval is the product as much as the mean is.

## 4. The backtest

- Walk-forward, monthly checkpoints May–September, 2023–2025, 4,892
  player-checkpoints. No information from after the checkpoint touches a
  projection — the leakage guards are listed in README and were chosen
  before seeing results.
- [Chart: results/hitters_rmse_by_checkpoint.png]
- Table: four systems (naive, league-shrink, PROOF, prior-only), RMSE
  PA-weighted and raw.
- What beating naive by ~29% means; the margin over prior-only is small
  *on purpose*: ROS wOBA is close to a hardest public forecasting target
  and honest edges live in the third decimal. Tuning discipline (grid on
  2023–24, single 2025 holdout, flat curve past 4×) is the difference
  between finding that edge and inventing it.

## 4b. Pitchers: same engine, different physics

- Components: K%, uBB+HBP%, HR% per batter faced; BF/IP; a heavily-shrunk
  ERA–FIP gap; role-specific (SP/RP) league means.
- The finding: the league-mean shrink beats the player-specific prior for
  pitchers (opposite of hitters). Three years of pitcher history decays —
  injuries, arsenals, role changes. The "players change" drift term is
  ~15× larger for pitchers (σ 0.23 FIP vs .015 wOBA) — same architecture,
  two instruments, one honest contrast.
- Engineering story worth one paragraph: BR rate-limited the build; the
  pitcher pipeline moved to pitch-level Statcast and was *validated against
  the pinned BR data* before use (corr ≥ 0.9999). Pitch-level store is now
  pre-positioned for phase-2 xwOBA features.
- [Chart: results/pitchers_fip_rmse_by_checkpoint.png]

## 5. Calibration — the plot this project exists for

- [Chart: results/hitters_calibration.png] 50/80/95% intervals covering
  49.3/80.6/95.5%. If PROOF says a player has an 80% chance to finish
  between X and Y, he's right 80% of the time. That's what makes it a
  forecast instead of a ranking.
- Pitchers: 52.1/80.5/93.9% on FIP after the drift term; the 95% interval
  running slightly narrow is itself a finding (tail outcomes = injuries).

## 6. Where the system is wrong, and why

- Rookies (league-average prior by design — show one example).
- Injuries and decline: the +0.004–0.007 wOBA overprojection bias, and why
  no outcome-based system can see a player playing hurt.
- Trades / park changes / role changes. 2020's 60-game season.
- [Chart: results/hitters_error_vs_pt.png — error concentrates where it should.]

## 7. What I'd add with proprietary data

- TrackMan/Hawk-Eye: bat tracking, contact point, approach angles —
  the in-season signal wOBA outcomes are too noisy to carry. (Spec's rule:
  say what you'd do with it; don't fake it.)
- Defensive positioning and quality-of-opposition adjustments.
