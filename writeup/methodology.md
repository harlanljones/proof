# PROOF: methodology and results

> Rest-of-season MLB wOBA and FIP/ERA forecasts, built in public. Every
> number below is regenerated by `make backtest` from pinned snapshots
> (`writeup/results/`); the full build trail, including every deviation
> from the original spec, is in [`spec-audit.md`](spec-audit.md).

## 1. The question

"Who will hit better over the rest of the season?" is a question every
front office answers daily and every public leaderboard answers badly —
usually by echoing the season-to-date line with a coat of paint. This
project is a public, reproducible answer to that question.

The one-sentence method: start from what a player did over the last three
years, then update it with what he's done this season — trusting the new
data exactly as much as its sample size earns, separately for each skill.

## 2. Why shrinkage

The 50-PA hot streak problem: two hitters, both .400 wOBA through 60 PA.
One is a career .380 hitter, one a career .290. They are not the same bet,
and any system that treats them the same is a ranking, not a forecast.

The fix is per-skill shrinkage, because different skills stabilize at
different speeds. Strikeouts speak in about 60 plate appearances; walks
need about 120; home runs about 170; doubles and triples never finish
speaking within a single season. PROOF carries a separate blend weight for
each component, borrowed from Carleton's stabilization constants and then
re-tuned on data (see §4), rather than one global "trust the hot streak"
dial.

## 3. The system

- **Prior.** A Marcel-style preseason projection: the last three seasons
  weighted 5/4/3, regressed toward the league mean with 1,200 PA of
  ballast, with the standard age adjustment. Computable in March, before
  a single pitch of the target season is thrown.
- **Update.** In-season, each event rate (the seven PA outcomes for
  hitters; K%, uBB+HBP%, HR% per batter faced for pitchers) is blended
  between the year-to-date observation and the prior with its own
  empirical-Bayes weight, then recombined using the season's actual wOBA
  weights.
- **Why event rates instead of BB%/K%/ISO/BABIP.** Those four don't add
  up to wOBA — the decomposition leaves a residual the model can't price.
  The seven PA outcomes do sum, so the projection is built where it will
  be scored. The spec-audit has the arithmetic.
- **Uncertainty.** Every projection ships with a 50/80/95% predictive
  interval, built from the residual spread plus an explicit "players
  change" drift term (§5). The interval is the product as much as the
  mean is.

## 4. The backtest

Walk-forward, monthly checkpoints from May through September, 2023–2025:
4,892 hitter player-checkpoints. No information from after the checkpoint
touches a projection — the leakage guards (priors use only prior seasons,
league regression targets only trailing seasons, write-once snapshots)
were fixed before any results were seen.

PA-weighted RMSE on rest-of-season wOBA, all checkpoints pooled:

| system | what it is | RMSE |
|---|---|---|
| naive | ROS = year-to-date, no shrinkage | .0572 |
| eb_league | shrink YTD toward league mean | .0437 |
| prior | preseason Marcel alone | .0411 |
| **proof** | shrink YTD toward player prior, per-component k | **.0405** |

PROOF beats its own prior in each individual season (2023: .0400 vs
.0409; 2024: .0422 vs .0423; 2025: .0392 vs .0400). The margin over
prior-only is small *on purpose*. Rest-of-season wOBA is close to the
hardest public forecasting target there is, and honest edges live in the
third decimal. What separates finding an edge from inventing one is
tuning discipline: the shrinkage multiplier was selected on 2023–24 only
(the grid is flat past 4×, so the choice is not knife-edge), then
validated once on the 2025 holdout the tuning loop never saw — proof
.0393 vs prior .0400. The backtest said trust the prior about 4× more
than the borrowed stabilization constants suggested; that multiplier is
the single most consequential number in the repo, and it was chosen
without ever looking at 2025.

## 4b. Pitchers: same engine, different physics

The pitcher side runs the identical architecture on per-batter-faced
components (K%, uBB+HBP%, HR%; BF/IP; a heavily-shrunk ERA–FIP gap) with
role-specific (SP/RP) league means. BF-weighted RMSE, 2023–2025:

| target | n | naive | prior | proof | eb_league |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FIP | 3,473 | 1.218 | 1.028 | 0.990 | **0.971** |
| ERA | 3,400 | 1.910 | 1.467 | 1.431 | **1.378** |

The honest finding: the player-specific prior beats naive and
prior-only clearly on both targets, but *loses to league-mean
shrinkage*. Three years of pitcher history decays fast — injuries,
arsenal changes, role conversions. The fitted "players change" drift term
is σ ≈ 0.23 FIP versus ≈ 0.015 wOBA for hitters — about 15× larger after
scale adjustment. Same engine, two instruments, one contrast that's
actually about baseball, not code.

One engineering story worth telling: mid-milestone, Baseball-Reference
429-banned the build IP. The pitcher pipeline moved to pitch-level
Statcast, reconstructed league/season lines, and validated them against
the pinned BR snapshots before use (correlation ≥ 0.9999,
`python -m ingest.validate_statcast`). After a cool-down the polite
prefetcher (30 s spacing) completed the BR set anyway, so the ERA
evaluation covers all three seasons — and the pitch-level store is now
pre-positioned for phase-2 xwOBA features. Getting rate-limited improved
the codebase.

## 5. Calibration — the plot this project exists for

A mean forecast you can't trust probabilistically is a ranking. The
uncertainty model is the strongest part of PROOF:

- **Hitters**, 4,892 checkpoints: nominal 50/80/95% intervals covered
  **49.3 / 80.6 / 95.5%**. If PROOF says a player has an 80% chance to
  finish between X and Y, he finishes there 80% of the time.
- **Pitchers (FIP)**, 3,473 checkpoints: **52.2 / 80.6 / 94.0%** after
  the drift term. The 95% interval running slightly narrow is itself a
  finding: the missing tail coverage is injured and role-changed arms —
  outcomes no rate-based system can see coming.
- **Pitchers (ERA)**, 3,400 checkpoints: **51.0 / 79.6 / 93.1%** after an
  ERA-specific drift term — defense, park and sequencing variance
  (~1.0 ERA of sd) that FIP can't see, estimated by method of moments on
  2023-24 training residuals only; the 2025 holdout independently implied
  1.03. Before it, the ERA interval (reusing the FIP sd) covered just
  37.1 / 64.0 / 82.5% — itself evidence of how much noise sits between
  FIP and ERA.

The calibration restored by the drift term is worth dwelling on: tuning
the shrinkage hard (trusting the prior 4×) improved RMSE but left the
intervals too narrow — the model was "right" on average and overconfident
in exactly the way that loses people games. The σ ≈ 0.015 wOBA drift term
prices irreducible in-season change (injury, role, talent) explicitly
instead of pretending it doesn't exist.

## 6. Where the system is wrong, and why

- **Rookies** project from a league-average prior by construction. The
  first 200 PA of a prospect's career is systematically underforecast.
- **Injuries and decline** drive a persistent +0.004–0.007 wOBA
  overprojection bias: the model sees a .340 player take 50 PA at .230
  and shrinks toward .340, because 50 PA earns almost no trust — but it
  also can't distinguish "small sample" from "playing hurt". No
  outcome-based system can; this is a fundamental limit, not a bug.
- **Trades, park changes, role changes** are invisible by design — the
  prior is the player, not the situation.
- **2020's 60-game season** enters priors with correctly reduced weight
  rather than being special-cased.

## 7. What I'd add with proprietary data

Per the spec's rule — say what you'd do with it, don't fake it:

- **TrackMan/Hawk-Eye bat tracking**: contact point, approach angles,
  swing decisions — the in-season signal that wOBA outcomes are too
  noisy to carry. This is where the +0.005 injury bias would actually
  get attacked: swing-speed deltas show up weeks before outcome rates.
- **Defensive positioning and quality-of-opposition adjustments**, for
  the ERA interval specifically — the missing variance in §5 is mostly
  defense the model can't see.

## 8. Reproduce it

```bash
git clone https://github.com/harlanljones/proof && cd proof
uv sync
make backtest          # hitters + pitchers walk-forward, pinned snapshots
```

Snapshots are tracked and write-once; the run is byte-identical to the
numbers quoted above. Everything changed en route from the original spec,
and why, is in [`spec-audit.md`](spec-audit.md).
